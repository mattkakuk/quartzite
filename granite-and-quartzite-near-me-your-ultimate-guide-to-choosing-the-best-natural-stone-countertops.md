## **2\. Demystifying Natural Stone: Granite vs. Quartzite**

Homeowners often ask us: _What is the real difference between granite and
quartzite?_ While both are 100% natural stones formed deep beneath the Earth
's crust over millions of years, they possess distinct personalities,
compositions, and visual styles.

+-------------+---------------+---------------+

| Feature | Natural Granite | Natural Quartzite |

+-------------+---------------+---------------+

| Rock Type | Igneous | Metamorphic |

| Mohs Hardness | 6 to 6.5 / 10 | 7 / 10 (Harder) |

| Primary Look | Flecked, Granular | Sweeping, Marble-Like |

| Heat Resistance | Outstanding | Outstanding |

| Acid Resistance | Very High | Very High |

+-------------+---------------+---------------+



### **What is Granite?**

Granite is an igneous rock formed from slowly cooling magma deep underground.
It is composed primarily of quartz, feldspar, mica, and other minerals, which
give it a classic, salt-and-pepper flecked texture or intricate swirling
patterns.

#### **Key Advantages of Granite Countertops**

  * **Incredible Variety:** Available in hundreds of colors, from rich midnight blacks and deep blues to warm golds and subtle whites.
  * **Proven Durability:** Highly resistant to heat, scratches, and daily kitchen impact.
  * **Budget-Friendly Luxury:** Offers exceptional value across various price tiers, making it accessible for almost any remodeling budget.

### **What is Quartzite?**

Quartzite is a metamorphic rock that starts its life as pure quartz sandstone.
Under immense heat and tectonic pressure deep within the earth, individual
quartz grains fuse into a dense, tightly interlocked stone matrix.

#### **Key Advantages of Quartzite Countertops**

  * **Marble-Like Elegance:** Offers the soft, flowing, luxurious veining of classic European marble without marble 's soft porosity.
  * **Extreme Hardness:** Ranks at a 7 on the Mohs Hardness Scale—making it harder than a steel knife blade.
  * **UV Stability:** 100% color-fast under sunlight, making it perfect for indoor and outdoor living spaces alike.

## **3\. The Geology Lesson: How Nature Crafts Your Countertop**

To truly appreciate your stone, it helps to understand how nature forged it.
Think of granite as a slow-baked recipe of mixed mineral crystals, and
quartzite as a pressure-cooked diamond in the rough.

### **The Heat and Pressure Matrix**

While granite cools slowly into a speckled mosaic, quartzite undergoes a
dramatic transformation called metamorphism. Imagine taking soft, granular
sand on a beach and pressing it so hard that the spaces between the grains
disappear entirely. That interlocking crystal structure gives quartzite its
legendary durability and glassy sheen.

### **Why Natural Stone Outperforms Man-Made Options**

Many homeowners ask if engineered quartz is better than natural stone.
Engineered quartz uses synthetic resin binders to hold crushed stone together.
Put a boiling pot on engineered resin, and it can scorch, melt, or discolor.
Natural quartzite and granite laugh in the face of heat because they were
literally born in volcanic or subterranean furnaces!

## **8\. Essential Care and Maintenance for Long-Lasting Surfaces**

One of the biggest myths about natural stone is that it requires high-
maintenance care. In reality, keeping granite and quartzite looking like new
takes just a few simple daily habits.

+------------------------+------------------------+

| DO THIS | AVOID THIS |

+------------------------+------------------------+

| Clean daily with warm water & soap | Harsh bleach, ammonia, or acid |

| Use soft microfiber towels | Abrasive wire pads or scrubbers |

| Wipe oil or wine spills promptly | Leaving citrus juice sitting long |

| Apply sealer every 12 to 24 mos. | Direct contact with raw harsh lye |

+------------------------+------------------------+



### **Simple Daily Cleaning**

Forget expensive specialized cleansers! Mild dish soap, warm water, and a soft
microfiber towel are all you need for daily cleanups. Buff the surface dry to
prevent water spots and reveal natural shine.

### **The Water-Drop Sealer Test**

Is it time to re-seal your counter? Splash a few drops of water on your
surface. If the water beads up into tight droplets, your seal is strong! If
the water soaks in and darkens the stone after 10 minutes, it is time for a
quick coat of impregnating sealer.

## **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)**

### **Q1: Is quartzite more expensive than granite?**

Generally, quartzite tends to sit in a higher price bracket than standard
granite. Because quartzite is denser and harder, quarrying, cutting, and
fabricating it requires specialized diamond tooling and additional labor.
However, entry-level quartzites can overlap in price with exotic, high-tier
granites.

### **Q2: Can I put hot pans directly on granite and quartzite counters?**

Yes! Both granite and quartzite possess exceptional natural heat tolerance
because they were formed under extreme thermal conditions underground. While
we always recommend using trivets or hot pads to protect sealers and prevent
thermal shock on cold days, brief contact with hot cookware will not scorch or
melt the stone.

### **Q3: How do I know if a stone is real quartzite or mislabeled marble?**

Real quartzite will easily scratch glass without dulling itself and will not
etch when exposed to household acids like lemon juice or vinegar. Soft marble,
on the other hand, will etch and dull under acidic contact. You can request an
acid test or scratch test on a sample scrap at our showroom to verify stone
purity.

### **Q4: How often should I seal my granite or quartzite countertops?**

Most granite and quartzite surfaces benefit from being sealed every 12 to 24
months. Highly porous lighter stones may need sealing once a year, while
extremely dense stones like Cristallo quartzite or dark granites may go two
years or longer between seal applications.

### **Q5: Will quartzite or granite scratch during daily kitchen prep?**

Granite scores between 6 and 6.5 on the Mohs Hardness Scale, and quartzite
scores a 7—making both stones harder than standard kitchen knife steel. While
slicing directly on your counter won't scratch the stone, it will quickly dull
your knives! We always suggest using wooden or plastic cutting boards to
protect your cutlery.
